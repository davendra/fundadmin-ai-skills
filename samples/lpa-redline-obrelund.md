## Summary of Changes

This redline reflects 15 negotiation items. Items marked **[NEW]** were not in the prior round. GP has pre-indicated willingness on Items 1–5; Items 6–15 remain open.

### Item 1: Carried Interest Rate

**Original:**
> "The General Partner shall be entitled to a Carried Interest equal to twenty-five percent (25%) of Net Profits allocable with respect to each LP's Capital Account after such LP has received distributions equal to its Invested Capital."

**Proposed:**
> "The General Partner shall be entitled to a Carried Interest equal to ~~twenty-five percent (25%)~~ **twenty percent (20%)** of Net Profits allocable with respect to each LP's Capital Account after such LP has received distributions equal to its Invested Capital."

**Counsel Note:** 25% carry with no preferred return is above market for a US buyout fund of this size ($2B target). ILPA guidance treats a preferred return as a core LP protection; we have not seen a sourced comparison supporting 25% without one. Our clients are unwilling to commit at 25% carry absent a meaningful hurdle rate. This is a threshold issue.

---

### Item 2: Preferred Return

**Original:**
> "There shall be no Preferred Return. Carried Interest shall accrue from the first dollar of profit."

**Proposed:**
> "~~There shall be no Preferred Return. Carried Interest shall accrue from the first dollar of profit.~~ **The General Partner shall not be entitled to any Carried Interest until each Limited Partner has received cumulative distributions equal to (i) the return of all Capital Contributions made by such Limited Partner plus (ii) a Preferred Return equal to eight percent (8%) per annum, compounded annually, on all Capital Contributions from the date each contribution is made until the date of return.**"

**Counsel Note:** An 8% preferred return (hurdle rate) is standard for buyout funds. The absence of any hurdle is the single most investor-unfriendly term in this LPA and was flagged by our clients' investment committees as a potential deal-breaker. We request the GP accept 8% pref with a standard 100% catch-up to follow.

### Item 3: Clawback — Joint and Several + Escrow

**Original:**
> "In the event a Clawback Amount is determined, the General Partner shall return to the Partnership such Clawback Amount. The obligation to fund a Clawback shall be borne solely by the General Partner entity and shall not constitute a personal obligation of any GP Principal."

**Proposed:**
> "In the event a Clawback Amount is determined, the General Partner shall return to the Partnership such Clawback Amount. ~~The obligation to fund a Clawback shall be borne solely by the General Partner entity and shall not constitute a personal obligation of any GP Principal.~~ **The obligation to fund a Clawback shall be joint and several among the General Partner and each GP Principal who received Carried Interest distributions. To secure performance of the clawback obligation, the General Partner shall maintain an escrow account holding no less than twenty-five percent (25%) of all Carried Interest distributions made to the General Partner and GP Principals, which escrow shall be released no earlier than two (2) years following the end of the Fund Term.**"

**Counsel Note:** A GP-entity-only clawback is unenforceable if the GP entity has no assets — the practical value of the clawback goes to zero. Joint-and-several liability with escrow is standard for ILPA-compliant funds. The 25% escrow is consistent with market practice.

### Item 4: Key Person Provision

**Original:**
> *[No Key Person provision exists in the current draft.]*

**Proposed (new section to be inserted as Section 6.5):**
> **"Section 6.5 — Key Person Event. The 'Key Persons' are Marcus J. Vennigrath and [one additional senior partner to be designated prior to First Close]. A 'Key Person Event' shall occur if any two Key Persons cease to devote substantially all of their business time to the Fund. Upon a Key Person Event: (i) the Investment Period shall be automatically suspended; (ii) the LPAC shall have sixty (60) days to vote whether to reinstate the Investment Period (requiring approval of LPAC members representing at least sixty-six and two-thirds percent (66⅔%) of LP commitments on the LPAC); and (iii) if reinstatement is not approved, the Investment Period shall terminate and the Fund shall enter wind-down mode."**

**Counsel Note:** The absence of any key person provision is a material omission. Institutional LPs are prohibited by investment policy from committing to funds without a key person clause. This is a non-negotiable requirement for our clients.

### Item 5: Organizational Expenses Cap

**Original:**
> "The Fund shall bear all Organizational Expenses incurred in connection with the formation and offering of the Fund, without limitation."

**Proposed:**
> "The Fund shall bear all Organizational Expenses incurred in connection with the formation and offering of the Fund~~, without limitation~~**, provided that the aggregate Organizational Expenses borne by the Fund shall not exceed Three Million Dollars ($3,000,000). Any Organizational Expenses in excess of such cap shall be borne by the General Partner**."

**Counsel Note:** Uncapped org expenses create a blank check for GP cost recovery. $3M is at the high end of market for a $2B fund but acceptable to our clients as a hard cap. Current draft has no cap whatsoever.

### Item 6: LPAC Consent for Affiliate Transactions [NEW]

**Original:**
> "The General Partner may enter into transactions with Affiliates of the General Partner on behalf of the Fund, provided such transactions are on arm's-length terms as determined by the General Partner in its sole discretion."

**Proposed:**
> "The General Partner may enter into transactions with Affiliates of the General Partner on behalf of the Fund **only with the prior written approval of the LPAC**, provided such transactions are on arm's-length terms ~~as determined by the General Partner in its sole discretion~~ **as determined by an independent third-party valuation firm appointed by the LPAC**."

**Counsel Note:** The current standard — GP self-certifying arm's-length terms — is circular and creates a clear conflict of interest. LPAC prior approval is standard market practice for affiliate transactions above a de minimis threshold.

### Item 7: Confidentiality Exceptions

**Original:**
> "Each Limited Partner agrees to keep confidential all information received from the General Partner or the Fund and not to disclose such information to any third party, except as required by applicable law."

**Proposed:**
> "Each Limited Partner agrees to keep confidential all information received from the General Partner or the Fund and not to disclose such information to any third party, except **(i)** as required by applicable law**, (ii) to such Limited Partner's investment advisors, consultants, auditors, and legal counsel who are bound by confidentiality obligations no less restrictive than those herein, (iii) to such Limited Partner's investment committee, board of trustees, or governing body, (iv) as required by such Limited Partner's public records laws or freedom of information obligations, and (v) in connection with such Limited Partner's regulatory examinations or audits by governmental authorities**."

**Counsel Note:** The current provision would prevent institutional LPs from sharing fund information with their own investment committees and auditors — this is untenable. Public pension funds are also subject to FOIA/public records laws; they cannot contractually waive those obligations.

### Item 8: Excuse and Exclusion Rights

**Original:**
> *[No excuse or exclusion rights provision exists in the current draft.]*

**Proposed (new section to be inserted as Section 7.4):**
> **"Section 7.4 — Excuse and Exclusion Rights. A Limited Partner may elect to be excused from (or, in the case of regulatory prohibition, excluded from) any specific investment if such investment would (i) cause such Limited Partner to violate applicable law or regulation, (ii) cause adverse tax consequences to such Limited Partner (including UBTI), (iii) violate such Limited Partner's investment mandate or policies, or (iv) constitute a prohibited transaction under ERISA. Excused or excluded Limited Partners shall not bear the costs of such investment but shall also not participate in its returns. The General Partner shall provide reasonable advance notice of proposed investments to allow timely election of excuse rights."**

**Counsel Note:** Excuse rights are critical for ERISA-plan investors and tax-exempt entities that may face UBTI from certain investment structures (e.g., operating companies held directly). The absence of this provision makes the Fund inaccessible to many institutional investors.

### Item 9: GP Assignment Restrictions

**Original:**
> "The General Partner may assign its interest as general partner to any Person, subject to obtaining the prior written consent of Limited Partners holding a majority of LP commitments."

**Proposed:**
> "The General Partner may assign its interest as general partner **only to an Affiliate of the General Partner that is controlled by the same Key Persons**, subject to obtaining the prior written consent of Limited Partners holding ~~a majority~~ **sixty-six and two-thirds percent (66⅔%)** of LP commitments."

**Counsel Note:** Unrestricted GP assignment (with only majority LP consent) enables the GP to sell the fund management business to a third party. Our clients are investing in the team, not the entity. Assignment should be restricted to controlled affiliates, and the consent threshold should be supermajority.

---

### Item 10: Management Fee Offset — 100%

**Original:**
> "Monitoring fees, transaction fees, and other fees received by the General Partner or its Affiliates from Portfolio Companies shall offset the Management Fee at a rate of fifty percent (50%)."

**Proposed:**
> "Monitoring fees, transaction fees, and other fees received by the General Partner or its Affiliates from Portfolio Companies shall offset the Management Fee at a rate of ~~fifty percent (50%)~~ **one hundred percent (100%)**."

**Counsel Note:** 50% fee offset is below market. ILPA Principles 3.0 recommend that fee income offset the management fee in full. The GP is essentially charging the portfolio companies (whose economic returns flow to LPs) and then keeping half of those fees — which is a form of double-charging. We require 100%.

### Item 11: No-Fault GP Removal

**Original:**
> "The General Partner may be removed for Cause only, upon the vote of Limited Partners holding at least eighty percent (80%) of LP commitments."

**Proposed:**
> "The General Partner may be removed ~~for Cause only~~ **with or without Cause**, upon the vote of Limited Partners holding at least ~~eighty percent (80%)~~ **seventy-five percent (75%)** of LP commitments. ~~[Cause definition remains unchanged.]~~ **In the case of removal without Cause, the General Partner shall be entitled to its accrued but unpaid Management Fees and its Carried Interest on unrealized gains as of the removal date, calculated on a mark-to-market basis by an independent appraiser appointed by the LPAC.**"

**Counsel Note:** Cause-only removal with an 80% threshold makes GP removal practically impossible. The 80% threshold means a single large LP (e.g., a 25% commitment LP) can single-handedly block removal. No-fault removal at 75% is market standard and is a basic governance right.

### Item 12: Successor GP Provisions [NEW]

**Original:**
> *[No successor GP provision exists.]*

**Proposed (new section to be inserted as Section 6.8):**
> **"Section 6.8 — Successor General Partner. If the General Partner is removed, resigns, or otherwise ceases to serve, the LPAC shall have the right, by vote of members representing at least sixty-six and two-thirds percent (66⅔%) of LP commitments on the LPAC, to (i) appoint a successor general partner, (ii) engage a third-party fund administrator to wind down the Fund, or (iii) vote to terminate and liquidate the Fund. Pending appointment of a successor, the LPAC shall have full authority to manage the Fund's ongoing affairs."**

**Counsel Note:** Without successor GP provisions, removal of the GP creates a governance vacuum. The LPAC needs explicit authority to act in that scenario.

### Item 13: Fund Term Reduction

**Original:**
> "The term of the Partnership shall be twelve (12) years from the Final Closing Date, subject to two (2) one-year extensions at the General Partner's sole discretion and two (2) additional one-year extensions with LPAC consent."

**Proposed:**
> "The term of the Partnership shall be ~~twelve (12)~~ **ten (10)** years from the Final Closing Date, subject to ~~two (2) one-year extensions at the General Partner's sole discretion and~~ two (2) one-year extensions with LPAC consent ~~[and two (2) additional one-year extensions with LPAC consent]~~."

**Counsel Note:** A 12-year base term (potentially extending to 16 years) is long even for a large-cap buyout fund. 10+2 (with consent) is market standard. Our clients' investment mandates generally limit fund terms to 12 years maximum; a potential 16-year fund creates policy compliance issues.

### Item 14: Most Favored Nation (MFN) for Large LPs [NEW]

**Original:**
> *[No MFN provision exists.]*

**Proposed (new section to be inserted as Section 9.6):**
> **"Section 9.6 — Most Favored Nation. Any Limited Partner with a Capital Commitment of One Hundred Million Dollars ($100,000,000) or more (an 'MFN Eligible LP') shall be entitled to elect, within thirty (30) days of the Final Closing Date, to receive the benefit of any more favorable economic terms granted to any other Limited Partner in any side letter or other agreement (excluding terms granted based on such other Limited Partner's unique regulatory or tax status). The General Partner shall provide each MFN Eligible LP with a summary of all side letter economic terms within fifteen (15) days of the Final Closing Date."**

**Counsel Note:** Our clients collectively represent $250M of commitments (12.5% of the Fund). At that commitment level, they have reasonable expectations of MFN rights. The GP currently has four side letters in place that our clients have not seen.

### Item 15: Quarterly Reporting Timeline

**Original:**
> "The General Partner shall provide quarterly reports to Limited Partners within ninety (90) days of the end of each fiscal quarter."

**Proposed:**
> "The General Partner shall provide quarterly reports to Limited Partners within ~~ninety (90)~~ **forty-five (45)** days of the end of each fiscal quarter. Such reports shall include, at minimum: (i) fund-level NAV and performance metrics (IRR, TVPI, DPI, RVPI), (ii) portfolio company updates, (iii) capital account statements, (iv) management fee calculations, and (v) a summary of any material events during the quarter."

**Counsel Note:** 90-day reporting is well below the ILPA standard of 45–60 days. Institutional LPs need timely data for their own quarterly reporting to boards and beneficiaries. We also propose codifying the minimum reporting contents — the current draft is silent on what the quarterly report must include.
